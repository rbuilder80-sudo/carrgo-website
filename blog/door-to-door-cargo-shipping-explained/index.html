Door to Door Cargo: What It Actually Means (And Why the Cheaper Quote Often Isn't)

Door-to-door freight means one provider coordinates collection from the origin address through to delivery at the destination address. Port-to-port covers only the terminal-to-terminal transport leg, so the two quotes should never be compared as if they include the same work.

Door-to-door vs port-to-port vs door-to-port

Port-to-port moves cargo between terminals. The shipper or consignee must separately arrange origin transport, export handling, import customs and destination delivery as applicable.

Door-to-port includes origin collection and the international transport leg, but the consignee takes over at the destination terminal.

Door-to-door coordinates collection, international freight, customs processes and final delivery under one shipment plan, subject to the agreed Incoterm and quotation scope.

Why the cheapest headline quote may not be cheaper

A low port-to-port figure can exclude exactly the services that become expensive when they are arranged late: customs brokerage, terminal handling, release fees and inland haulage. Storage and demurrage can also arise if the next step is not ready when the cargo arrives.

The correct comparison is total cost to the actual destination, with the same assumptions and exclusions.

Who benefits most from door-to-door cargo

Door-to-door is especially useful for importers that do not want to coordinate multiple providers, ecommerce businesses working to fulfilment deadlines, manufacturers protecting production schedules and first-time importers that need clear ownership of the whole movement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is door-to-door always more expensive than port-to-port?

The headline rate is usually higher because it covers more services. The relevant comparison is the total cost after customs, handling and inland delivery are added to a port-to-port move.

What happens if a door-to-door shipment is delayed?

The forwarder coordinates the affected leg and should provide one point of contact for the shipment, although the underlying delay can still be caused by carriers, ports, customs or external disruption.

Does door-to-door include customs at both ends?

It can, depending on the Incoterm, origin-country rules and quoted scope. Confirm export and import clearance explicitly before booking.

Can a single pallet move door-to-door?

Yes. Door-to-door describes the scope of the journey, not the shipment size. It can apply to LCL, air freight, road freight and full containers.

When should I choose door-to-port instead?

It can work well if you already have a trusted customs broker and haulier at destination and want them to manage the import-side work.
