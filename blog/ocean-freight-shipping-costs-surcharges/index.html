Ocean Freight Shipping: Why the Quoted Rate Is Rarely the Final Cost

The base ocean rate is only one part of a container or LCL movement. Terminal charges, fuel adjustments, seasonal surcharges, customs work and inland delivery can materially change the total cost.

What makes up an ocean freight cost

  • Base freight: the carrier charge for the ocean transport.
  • BAF or fuel adjustment: a mechanism used by carriers to reflect fuel-cost changes.
  • THC: terminal handling charges at origin and/or destination.
  • PSS: peak season surcharge applied when demand and capacity conditions justify it.
  • Currency adjustment: some trades use currency-related factors.
  • Congestion or operational surcharges: temporary charges that may apply to specific ports or market conditions.

These are standard freight cost components, but the quote should state clearly which are included and which remain variable.

How a cheaper rate can become the more expensive shipment

A provider can quote a low base rate while excluding terminal, documentation, customs and final delivery charges. Another provider may show a higher number because more of the movement is already included. Without normalising the scope, the cheaper figure can be misleading.

Three questions to ask before accepting an ocean freight quote

  1. Which carrier and terminal surcharges are included in this figure?
  2. Is customs clearance included, and what government taxes or duties remain outside the freight quote?
  3. Does the price end at the port, or does it include delivery to the final address?

Compare the total transport scope

Ask for a written list of assumptions, validity period, free time and exclusions. Ocean rates can move quickly, so transparency is more useful than a promise that every charge will remain fixed indefinitely.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is BAF in ocean freight?

BAF is a bunker or fuel adjustment factor used to reflect changes in marine fuel costs.

Why can an ocean freight invoice be higher than the first quote?

The final amount can rise if the first figure excluded terminal charges, surcharges, customs work, delivery or if shipment details and carrier costs changed before booking.

Are ocean freight surcharges negotiable?

Some charges are carrier or terminal driven and may not be negotiable. The key is to know which charges apply before committing.

Does Carrgo include all surcharges upfront?

Carrgo aims to identify applicable freight and handling charges in the quotation and clearly state any variable or excluded items rather than presenting only a base ocean rate.

How can I avoid unexpected ocean freight costs?

Compare like-for-like scope, confirm quote validity, check surcharges and destination charges, and make sure customs and final delivery responsibilities are explicit.
