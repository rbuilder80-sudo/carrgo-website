Operational advisory, not a numerical congestion ranking

No comparable, timestamped primary measurement was verified this week for port-wide waiting times, vessel queues, berth utilisation or congestion scores. Unknown does not mean normal: it means the evidence needed to calculate a defensible ranking was unavailable.

Weekly evidence summary

Liverpool published weekend Langton Lock closures for 11–12 October and a Gladstone Lock closure for 15 October. Belfast scheduled a D3 dredging programme from 12 October to 5 January and warned that VTS may introduce one-way traffic when required. Dublin’s capital-dredging notice remains within its approximate two-week period, without a confirmed real-time stage or completion date. The latest Met Office forecast carries gale warnings for Malin and Shannon, while relevant coastal sectors have strong-wind warnings. These are local operating constraints and weather context—not measurements of port-wide congestion.

What changed over the past seven days

  • Liverpool added three scheduled closure windows. Notices 67 and 68 were published on 6 October, adding Langton Lock closures on 11–12 October and a Gladstone Lock closure on 15 October. These replace the previous week’s expired local windows; they do not establish a Liverpool-wide delay.
  • Belfast published a longer-running dredging programme. Notice 24 sets a 12 October to 5 January work period and explicitly allows Belfast VTS to introduce one-way traffic when required. The notice does not say that one-way traffic is continuously active.
  • Belfast added a vessel-specific exclusion-zone notice. Notice 25 scheduled HMS Queen Elizabeth’s arrival on 7 October. Because no departure time is stated, the current status is unknown and should be checked with Belfast VTS.
  • Dublin’s previously announced dredging period continues. Notice 49 still describes an approximate two-week programme from 1 October. Approximate duration is not evidence of real-time progress or a confirmed completion date.
  • Marine weather strengthened. Compared with 2 October, when the reviewed shipping forecast had no gale warnings in force, the 9 October forecast carries warnings for Malin and Shannon and relevant coastal sectors retain strong-wind warnings. That change does not prove port congestion worsened.

No numerical week-on-week congestion change is calculated because there are no comparable verified port-wide measurements for either week.

Questions to ask before booking or collection

  1. Does the Liverpool vessel plan use Langton Lock on 11–12 October or Gladstone Lock on 15 October?
  2. Is Hornby Passage suitable for the vessel, and has the Dockmaster confirmed the alternative route?
  3. Will Dublin’s active work area affect the planned berth, manoeuvring or terminal cut-off?
  4. Has Belfast VTS introduced one-way traffic for the D3 work, and is the D1 exclusion zone still active?
  5. Has the sailing status, terminal cut-off or vehicle appointment changed since confirmation?
  6. When does free time begin, and who bears demurrage, detention or storage if the plan changes?

Methodology and limitations

Carrgo reviews dated public primary sources and records each supported claim at the narrowest defensible level. Port or terminal notices support only the location and period they name. Weather forecasts describe sea or coastal areas and are not treated as evidence of terminal queues. Carrgo does not currently use a verified AIS feed and does not calculate a numerical congestion, health, queue or berth-utilisation score without traceable measurements and a reproducible method. Missing values remain unknown. Advice is separated from facts, and users should confirm time-sensitive arrangements with the relevant carrier, terminal, port or VTS.
