Weekly evidence summary

Liverpool added two Langton Lock closure windows for 27 and 28 September and amended its South Gladstone notice to extend the affected quay closure to 2 October. Dublin Port scheduled a capital-dredging campaign from 1 October for approximately two weeks. Belfast’s archive added a turbidity-buoy notice, but its operational detail was not available from the linked public page. Current Met Office forecasts carry no formal gale warning for the Irish Sea or Malin, while relevant coastal areas retain strong-wind warnings. These are local operating constraints and weather context—not measurements of port-wide congestion.

What changed over the past seven days

  • Liverpool published new weekend lock restrictions. Notice 65 adds two dated Langton Lock closure windows on 27 and 28 September. This is the clearest new scheduling constraint for early-week Liverpool movements.
  • South Gladstone’s restriction was extended. Amended Notice 57 moves the stated end from 27 September to 19:00 on 2 October. This supersedes the earlier end date used in the 18 September report.
  • Dublin published a forward dredging notice. Notice 49 schedules capital dredging from 1 October for approximately two weeks. The approximate duration is not a confirmed completion date.
  • Belfast added Notice 22. Its title identifies turbidity-buoy deployment for D3 works, but public operational detail remained unavailable, so no positions or effects are inferred.
  • Relevant gale warnings cleared. The Irish Sea and Malin have no formal gale warning in the current shipping forecast, although coastal strong-wind warnings remain. That change does not prove congestion improved.

No numerical week-on-week congestion change is calculated because there are no comparable verified port-wide measurements for either week.

Questions to ask before booking or collection

  1. Does the vessel plan use Langton Lock during either 27–28 September closure window?
  2. Does the South Gladstone or Royal Seaforth restricted quay affect the nominated berth or collection route?
  3. Will Dublin’s planned dredging area affect the vessel call after 1 October?
  4. Has the sailing status, terminal cut-off or vehicle appointment changed since confirmation?
  5. Are customs release, port health or transit documents complete before dispatch?
  6. When does free time begin, and who bears demurrage, detention or storage if the plan changes?

Methodology and limitations

Carrgo reviews dated public primary sources and records each supported claim at the narrowest defensible level. Port or terminal notices support only the location and period they name. Weather forecasts describe sea or coastal areas and are not treated as evidence of terminal queues. Carrgo does not currently use a verified AIS feed and does not calculate a numerical congestion, health, queue or berth-utilisation score without traceable measurements and a reproducible method. Missing values remain unknown. Advice is separated from facts, and users should confirm time-sensitive arrangements with the relevant carrier, terminal, port or VTS.
