What changed over the past seven days

  • Liverpool added a new navigational notice. Notice 69 records C11 removed for dredging, with Vox Alexia expected around 26 October and the mark expected off station until mid-November. This is a local navigational advisory, not evidence of a Liverpool-wide queue.
  • The Gladstone Lock window passed. Notice 68’s scheduled 15 October closure period is no longer current. The public index does not confirm reopening, so collection and sailing plans should be checked directly rather than inferred from the elapsed time.
  • Dublin’s dredging campaign reached its approximate stated duration. Notice 49 describes approximately two weeks from 1 October, but no completion notice or real-time work stage was verified. Do not treat the planned duration as proof that work has ended.
  • Belfast’s local restrictions remain relevant. Notice 24 continues D3 dredging through 5 January 2027 and permits one-way traffic when required. Notice 26’s scheduled departure period passed without a completion confirmation on the public archive.
  • Marine weather changed, but not measured congestion. The 9 October review carried gale warnings for Malin and Shannon; the 16 October forecast has no formal gale heading for those areas, while North Channel inshore strong-wind warnings remain. This does not establish that port operations improved or worsened.

No numerical week-on-week congestion change is calculated because there are no comparable verified port-wide measurements for either week.

Implications for importers and collection planning

  1. Ask the carrier or port VTS to confirm the exact lock, berth and approach for the planned call.
  2. Request the current terminal cut-off and vehicle appointment after any weather or navigational notice changes.
  3. Confirm who bears storage, demurrage or detention if a vessel or collection window moves.
  4. Keep a shipment-specific contingency route; local notices do not prove a port-wide delay, but they can affect an individual call.

Methodology and limitations

Carrgo reviews dated public primary sources and records each supported claim at the narrowest defensible level. Port or terminal notices support only the location and period they name. Weather forecasts describe sea or coastal areas and are not treated as evidence of terminal queues. Carrgo does not currently use a verified AIS feed and does not calculate a numerical congestion, health, queue or berth-utilisation score without traceable measurements and a reproducible method. Missing values remain unknown. Advice is separated from facts, and users should confirm time-sensitive arrangements with the relevant carrier, terminal, port or VTS.
