What's Actually Included in Freight Forwarding Services

Freight forwarding is more than booking space on a ship or aircraft. The real value is coordinating each handoff — from supplier collection and paperwork to customs clearance and final delivery.

Step 1: A quote that reflects the real scope

A useful quote should define the complete movement: origin collection, international freight, handling, customs work and final delivery where required. A cheap port-to-port figure can look attractive but is not directly comparable with a true door-to-door quote.

Use our freight quote form to provide the shipment details needed for an accurate comparison.

Step 2: Choosing the right mode

For many China-to-UK pallet shipments, sea freight is the cost-effective default. If stock is urgently needed, air freight may be worth the higher cost. A forwarder should explain the trade-off rather than simply book the first mode requested.

See our China to UK freight guide for route-specific options.

Step 3: Documentation and export preparation

Commercial invoices, packing lists and any origin or product-specific documents need to be accurate before departure. Packaging also matters: goods prepared for domestic handling may need stronger protection for international transport and repeated loading operations.

Step 4: Customs clearance

Every UK import needs the right declaration and supporting information. Certain commodities can also be subject to additional UK product, sanitary, safety or licensing controls. Carrgo can coordinate customs clearance as part of the shipment rather than leaving the importer to source an unrelated broker at the last minute.

Step 5: Final delivery and tracking

Arrival at Felixstowe, Southampton, London Gateway or an airport is not the end of the movement. Cargo still needs release, inland transport and final delivery. A properly scoped freight forwarding service manages these handoffs and keeps the shipper informed if timings change.

What to check before choosing a freight forwarder

  • Is the price port-to-port or genuinely door-to-door?
  • Are customs clearance and destination charges included?
  • Who is your named point of contact?
  • How are delays and exceptions communicated?
  • Does the forwarder understand the exact origin-destination lane?

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a freight forwarder and a shipping line?

A shipping line operates vessels. A freight forwarder arranges the full cargo journey using carriers and hauliers, while coordinating documentation, customs and delivery.

Do freight forwarding services include customs clearance?

It varies by provider. Carrgo can include customs clearance as part of the same managed movement.

How far in advance should I book freight?

Lead time depends on route, mode, capacity and season. Sea freight generally needs more planning than air freight.

Can freight forwarders handle Northern Ireland movements?

Yes, but GB–NI freight has route-specific customs requirements that need to be planned correctly.

What documents are normally needed?

A commercial invoice and packing list are common starting points. Additional documents depend on the goods, origin, Incoterm and applicable controls.
