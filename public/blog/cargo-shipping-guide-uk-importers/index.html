Cargo Shipping: What It Actually Means and How to Get It Right

Cargo shipping can mean anything from a pallet to a full container. For commercial imports and exports, the important distinction is that freight usually involves multiple transport legs, customs formalities and final delivery — not simply sending a parcel.

The four ways cargo actually moves

Sea freight (FCL/LCL) is usually the default for heavy or high-volume cargo where speed is not the priority. Full Container Load (FCL) gives one shipper exclusive use of a container; Less than Container Load (LCL) shares container space and cost with other shippers.

Air freight is used when time matters more than cost. Door-to-door air freight can include collection, airline handling, customs clearance and final delivery under one managed booking.

Road freight covers UK domestic and European movements and is also the final leg for many sea and air shipments.

Rail freight can provide a middle ground on selected Eurasian routes where sea is too slow and air is too expensive.

The part most people underestimate: customs

Every commercial shipment entering the UK must meet the relevant customs requirements. The exact paperwork depends on the goods, origin, value, Incoterm and any product-specific controls. Missing or incorrect documents can lead to holds, storage charges and delivery delays.

That is why customs clearance should be planned before the cargo reaches the UK, not treated as an afterthought after arrival.

Ireland and Northern Ireland need route-specific planning

Movements between Great Britain, Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland have their own customs and documentary requirements. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between cargo shipping and courier delivery?

Courier services mainly handle individual parcels. Cargo shipping handles commercial freight such as pallets, containers and bulk goods, often with customs clearance and multiple transport legs.

How long does cargo shipping take?

Transit time depends on origin, destination, transport mode, customs and current carrier schedules. Air is usually measured in days, while long-haul sea freight is normally measured in weeks.

Is customs clearance included?

Carrgo can manage UK customs clearance as part of the same freight movement, so it does not need to be arranged as a separate unmanaged step.
